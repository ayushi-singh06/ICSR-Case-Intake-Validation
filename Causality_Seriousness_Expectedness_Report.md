# TASK 4: CAUSALITY, SERIOUSNESS AND EXPECTEDNESS

## Pharmacovigilance Project
Product: Velunex (Fictional Training Product)

## 1. Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is the science of detecting, assessing, understanding and preventing adverse drug reactions.

This task focuses on:
1. Causality assessment
2. Seriousness assessment
3. Expectedness assessment
4. Missing information and follow-up questions

## 2. Expectedness Reference

According to the supplied fictional Velunex reference, the following adverse reactions are expected:

- Transient headache
- Nausea
- Dizziness
- Mild self-limiting rash

Other events are classified as unexpected for this exercise.

## 3. Causality Assessment Method

WHO-UMC causality assessment is used to evaluate the possible relationship between a suspected medicine and an adverse event.

Categories include:
- Certain
- Probable/Likely
- Possible
- Unlikely
- Conditional/Unclassified
- Unassessable/Unclassifiable

## 4. Case Assessment

### Case PV-201: Dizziness

Reported facts:
- Event: Dizziness
- Onset: 2 hours after first use
- Outcome: Resolved the same day
- No treatment or hospitalisation reported.

Causality: Possible
Reason: The timing is compatible with product use, but alternative causes and medical history are unknown.

Seriousness: Non-serious on supplied facts.

Severity: Not specified.

Expectedness: Expected.

Reason: Dizziness is listed in the Velunex reference.

Missing information:
- Dose and route
- Medical history
- Concomitant medicines
- Exact dates
- Patient and reporter details

### Case PV-202: Appendicitis

Reported facts:
- Event: Appendicitis
- Onset: 3 weeks after first use
- Hospitalisation required
- Medical history incomplete.

Causality: Possible, with substantial uncertainty.

Reason: The event occurred after product use, but timing alone cannot establish causality.

Seriousness: Serious.

Criterion: Hospitalisation.

Severity: Not specified.

Expectedness: Unexpected.

Reason: Appendicitis is not listed in the supplied reference.

Missing information:
- Complete medical history
- Diagnostic investigations
- Treatment details
- Concomitant medicines
- Dose and route
- Exact dates

Reporting implication: Handle as a serious unexpected event and follow applicable reporting procedures.

Causality: Possible.

Severity: Mild.

Missing information:

## 6. Important Observations

1. Seriousness and severity are different concepts.
2. Hospitalisation is a seriousness criterion.
3. Expectedness does not prove causality.
4. Temporal association alone does not prove that a medicine caused an event.
5. Missing information should never be invented.
6. Unknown concomitant medicines should not be recorded as none.

## 7. Conclusion

PV-201 and PV-203 are expected and non-serious according to the supplied case information.

PV-202 and PV-204 are unexpected and serious because hospitalisation or emergency admission was reported.

All four cases have a provisional WHO-UMC classification of Possible due to limited information.

Further follow-up is required to obtain missing clinical details and assess the cases more accurately.
